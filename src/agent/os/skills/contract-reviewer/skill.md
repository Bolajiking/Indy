# Skill: Contract Reviewer

## What This Skill Does

The Contract Reviewer analyzes brand partnership contracts and influencer agreements to identify red flags, clarify obligations, and produce specific negotiation recommendations. It helps creators understand what they are actually agreeing to — in plain language — and empowers them to push back on unfavorable terms with confidence.

This skill does not provide legal advice. It provides creator-business intelligence: pattern recognition against known industry norms, identification of terms that are non-standard or creator-unfavorable, and practical guidance on what to request in response.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Trigger this skill when a creator asks:
- "Can you review this contract?"
- "Is this deal fair?"
- "What should I look out for in this agreement?"
- "Does this contract have any red flags?"
- "What does [clause] mean?"
- "Should I sign this?"
- "How do I negotiate this contract?"

## Expertise Areas

### Red Flags in Creator Contracts

The following terms are creator-unfavorable and should always be flagged:

**Perpetual Usage Rights (Red Flag)**
A brand retaining the right to use your content forever, across any medium, without additional compensation. Standard acceptable usage rights are 6–12 months, limited to specified channels. Perpetual rights should trigger a significant usage rights fee or be rejected.

**Unlimited Revisions (Red Flag)**
If the contract allows the brand to request unlimited rounds of revisions without additional compensation, this is a major issue. Industry standard is 1–2 rounds of revisions. Anything beyond should be billable at an agreed hourly or flat rate.

**Kill Fees (Yellow Flag)**
Some contracts allow brands to cancel content after it is produced without paying the full rate. An acceptable kill fee is 50% of the total deal value. Anything below 25% is inadequate. No kill fee provision at all means the creator bears all risk if the brand decides not to proceed.

**Non-Compete Clauses (Red Flag — context dependent)**
Non-competes restricting the creator from working with competing brands during AND after the campaign period can be extremely limiting. Key questions: How broad is the competitive category? How long does the restriction last? Is it compensated? 30-day category exclusivity during the campaign is standard; 90-day+ post-campaign non-competes require significant premium compensation.

**Payment on Delivery vs. Net-60+ (Yellow/Red Flag)**
"Payment on delivery" sounds good but often means payment is withheld until the brand approves the content — which can be indefinitely delayed. Standard recommended terms: 50% upfront, 50% upon delivery. Net-30 is acceptable. Net-60 is a yellow flag. Net-90 is a red flag — cash flow risk is high.

**Exclusivity Without a Defined Category (Red Flag)**
Vague exclusivity language like "no competing brands" without defining what "competing" means gives the brand unlimited power to claim any future deal violates the contract. Exclusivity must be scoped to a specific, defined product category.

**IP Ownership Transfer (Red Flag)**
If the contract states the brand owns all created content and all intellectual property rights in perpetuity, the creator loses all control over their own work. Creators should retain IP ownership and license specific usage rights to the brand — not assign them.

**Morality Clauses (Yellow Flag)**
These give brands the right to terminate and potentially claw back payment if the creator does something deemed "disparaging" to the brand. Read the scope carefully. Overly broad morality clauses can be weaponized. Acceptable: restricts actively disparaging the brand. Unacceptable: restricts any controversial content or opinions unrelated to the brand.

### Standard Acceptable Terms

- Usage rights: 6–12 months, specified channels, no broadcast/TV without separate negotiation
- Revisions: 1–2 rounds included, additional revisions billable
- Payment: 50% upfront, 50% on delivery OR net-30 after delivery
- Exclusivity: 30-day category exclusivity during and 30 days post-campaign
- Kill fee: minimum 50% of total deal value
- Deliverables: clearly specified (format, length, platform, posting date, disclosure requirements)
- FTC disclosure: all contracts should require proper #ad or #sponsored disclosure; absence is a red flag

---

### FTC Disclosure Requirements

All creator contracts should include FTC disclosure language. Creators should be required (and protected) by the contract to disclose the relationship. Contracts that omit disclosure requirements may expose the creator to compliance risk. The disclosure must be clear and conspicuous — "Thanks to [Brand] for sponsoring this video" is standard for YouTube; #ad at the beginning of a post caption is standard for Instagram.

---

### What "Deliverables" Should Specify

A good deliverables section includes:
- Exact content format (e.g., "one 60-second Instagram Reel")
- Platform (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, etc.)
- Minimum duration if video
- Posting date or date range
- Caption requirements
- Link requirements (bio link, swipe-up, description link)
- Hashtag requirements
- Brand approval window (how long the brand has to review before posting)
- Number of revision rounds included

Vague deliverables ("one piece of content") are a yellow flag — they invite scope creep.

---

### Payment Structure Best Practices

- Always request 50% upfront before any work begins
- Define what triggers the remaining 50% payment clearly (delivery, posting, or brand approval)
- Include late payment penalties (1.5% per month is standard)
- Specify the payment method and currency
- International deals: specify currency and who bears FX risk

## How to Reason About This Skill

1. Read the contract in full, section by section.
2. Identify all clauses related to: usage rights, exclusivity, payment terms, deliverables, IP ownership, revisions, termination, non-compete, and kill fees.
3. Rate each clause as Green (standard/favorable), Yellow (watch/negotiate), or Red (problematic/reject or renegotiate).
4. For each Yellow and Red clause, provide the specific text at issue and a concrete recommended alternative or negotiation position.
5. Summarize the overall deal risk level and a go/no-go recommendation.

## Self-Improvement Notes

- This skill does not provide legal advice. Always recommend creators consult an entertainment or IP attorney for high-value deals (over $10,000).
- New contract clause types may emerge as platform features evolve (e.g., AI training data rights are an emerging red flag to watch for).
- If a creator reports that a brand rejected a negotiation point, log it — this informs future calibration of what's actually negotiable in different brand tiers.
- Watch for contracts using AI-generated content clauses that transfer rights to brand-generated AI derivatives of the creator's likeness or style.
