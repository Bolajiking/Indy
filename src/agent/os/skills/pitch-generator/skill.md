# Skill: Pitch Generator

## What This Skill Does

The Pitch Generator writes compelling, personalized outreach emails for creators to send to brands they want to partner with. It produces complete email drafts — subject line, body, and a multi-touch follow-up sequence — that are grounded in best practices for creator-to-brand outreach.

A great pitch is not a template. It is specific to the brand, demonstrates genuine understanding of what the brand is trying to achieve, and makes the creator's value proposition undeniable. This skill produces pitches that feel hand-crafted, not mass-produced.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Trigger this skill when a creator asks:
- "Write me a pitch for [brand]"
- "Help me reach out to [brand]"
- "Draft an email to [company] about a partnership"
- "How do I approach [brand] for a deal?"
- "I want to pitch [brand] — can you help?"
- "Write a cold outreach for a sponsorship"

---

## Expertise Areas

### What Makes a Winning Creator Pitch

The best pitches share these qualities:
1. **Specificity**: References the brand's actual products, campaigns, or values — not generic praise.
2. **Clear value proposition**: States what the creator brings (audience size, engagement, niche authority) without burying it.
3. **Social proof**: Includes one concrete achievement (past deal, engagement stat, case study).
4. **Conciseness**: Hits the key points in 200–350 words. Longer pitches get skimmed or ignored.
5. **Single, clear CTA**: Ends with one action — a call, a reply, a deck review — not multiple asks.

### Email Subject Line Optimization

The subject line determines whether the email gets opened. Best performers:
- Reference the brand name + a specific hook: "Partnership idea for [Brand]'s summer campaign"
- Use curiosity without being clickbait: "Your [product] + my 240K subscribers"
- Keep it under 50 characters for mobile readability
- Avoid spam triggers: "FREE," "URGENT," excessive punctuation, all caps

Avoid vague subjects like "Collaboration inquiry" or "Working together?" — these are skipped.

### Pitch Length and Structure

Optimal pitch length is 200–350 words. Structure:

1. **Hook (1–2 sentences)**: Open with something specific about the brand. Reference a recent campaign, product launch, or stated brand value.
2. **Who you are (2–3 sentences)**: Introduce the creator with the most relevant stat for this brand (audience size, niche, engagement rate, past relevant deal).
3. **Why this fit (2–3 sentences)**: Explain the audience overlap and content fit. Be specific — "My audience is 68% women aged 25–34 who actively shop for skincare" not "my audience loves beauty."
4. **The ask / proposal (1–2 sentences)**: Propose a specific format (one YouTube integration, a 3-post Instagram series, etc.) — not an open-ended "I'd love to work together."
5. **CTA (1 sentence)**: One clear next step. "Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week?" or "I've attached a one-pager with more details — happy to send a full media kit if useful."

### Personalization Techniques

- Reference the brand's most recent major campaign by name if known.
- Mention a brand value from their website ("I noticed [Brand] is committed to sustainability — my audience cares deeply about this too").
- If they recently launched a product, lead with it.
- Use the recipient's first name in the opener if known.
- Research whether the brand has a creator partnerships page or a specific team — address that team.

### Tone by Brand Type

- **DTC startup**: Casual, energetic, peer-to-peer tone. They want authenticity.
- **Enterprise / Fortune 500**: Professional but not stiff. Show ROI awareness.
- **VC-backed growth-stage brand**: Lead with metrics and scale. They think in growth KPIs.
- **Agency representing a brand**: More formal; agencies want to de-risk. Emphasize reliability, past brand work, content quality.

## How to Reason About This Skill

1. Identify the target brand and what they sell / who they're trying to reach.
2. Look for alignment between the creator's audience and the brand's customer profile.
3. Find a specific, genuine hook to open with (recent campaign, product, mission statement).
4. Draft the pitch in the correct length and structure for the brand type.
5. Write the subject line last — after the body is set, the best subject line becomes obvious.
6. Write three follow-up messages: Day 3, Day 7, Day 14 — each shorter than the last, each adding a small new piece of value.

---

## Common Patterns

- The Day 3 follow-up should add a relevant link (a past brand integration video, a media kit).
- The Day 7 follow-up should be a one-liner: "Wanted to bump this up in case it got buried."
- The Day 14 follow-up is the "break-up email" — polite closure that often triggers a response.
- Creators with past brand work should always include it. Even one reference client changes the pitch's credibility.
- If the creator has no past brand deals, lead with engagement rate and niche authority instead.

## Output Format

```
SUBJECT LINE: [Subject line here]

EMAIL BODY:
[Full pitch email — 200–350 words]

---

FOLLOW-UP SEQUENCE:

Follow-up 1 (Day 3):
[Short email — 80–100 words — adds value, references attachment or link]

Follow-up 2 (Day 7):
[One or two sentences — friendly bump]

Follow-up 3 (Day 14):
[Break-up email — 50–70 words — polite, leaves door open]
```

## Self-Improvement Notes

- If the creator reports that a pitch led to a deal, note what elements worked.
- Pitches to agencies require more formality and metrics-focus than pitches direct to brands.
- Always check whether the brand has a creator submission form — if so, recommend using it alongside the direct email.
- Avoid overusing the word "authentic" — it has become meaningless in creator pitches.
- Refresh follow-up timing guidance if industry norms shift (some verticals respond faster/slower).
