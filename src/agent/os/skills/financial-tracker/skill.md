# Skill: Financial Tracker

## What This Skill Does

The Financial Tracker helps creators understand, record, and reason about their creator business finances. It produces monthly snapshots of income and expenses, tracks outstanding invoices, flags cash flow risks, and surfaces key financial health indicators that creators — who are often not trained in business finance — need to stay solvent and profitable.

This skill bridges the gap between "I made some money this month" and "I understand my creator business financials." It is proactive, diagnostic, and always actionable.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Trigger this skill when a creator asks:
- "How much did I make this month?"
- "Track this payment / invoice"
- "What are my expenses?"
- "Am I profitable?"
- "What do I owe in taxes?"
- "When are my invoices due?"
- "I got paid $X from [brand] — log it"
- "What's my revenue this quarter?"
- "Help me understand my cash flow"
- "Do I have any unpaid invoices?"

## Expertise Areas

### What Creators Should Track

**Income Categories:**
- Brand deal / sponsorship payments (one-time or recurring)
- Affiliate commissions (by platform/program)
- Platform payments (YouTube AdSense, TikTok Creator Fund, Instagram bonuses)
- Membership / Patreon / subscription revenue
- Digital product sales (courses, templates, e-books)
- Licensing fees (content syndication, music licensing)
- Speaking fees / consulting revenue
- Merchandise revenue (gross, before COGS)

**Expense Categories:**
- Equipment (cameras, microphones, lighting, computers) — often depreciable assets
- Software subscriptions (editing tools, scheduling tools, analytics tools)
- Contractors (editors, VAs, designers, social media managers)
- Travel (for brand trips, events, content shoots)
- Props and production costs
- Home office / studio (rent, utilities if used for business)
- Professional services (accountant, attorney fees)
- Platform fees (marketplace commissions, payment processing fees)
- Marketing and advertising (promoting own content or products)
- Education (courses, books, conferences)

### Tax Considerations for Creators

Creators are self-employed business owners. Key tax obligations:
- **Quarterly estimated taxes**: Due January 15, April 15, June 15, September 15. Creators should set aside 25–30% of net income for federal taxes.
- **Self-employment tax**: 15.3% on net self-employment income (covers Social Security + Medicare).
- **Business deductions**: All legitimate business expenses reduce taxable income. Equipment, software, home office (if used exclusively for business), travel for content creation are all potentially deductible.
- **1099s**: Brands paying creators $600+ in a year are required to issue a 1099-NEC. Creators should track all payments, not just 1099s received.
- **Retirement accounts**: SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) are tax-advantaged options for self-employed creators — highly recommended.

Always recommend creators work with an accountant experienced in self-employment income once monthly revenue exceeds $3,000.

### Cash Flow Planning

Brand deals frequently pay on net-30 to net-90 terms. This creates a timing mismatch that can cause cash flow crises even when a creator is profitable on paper.

Key cash flow management principles:
- Always request 50% upfront on brand deals to reduce collection risk
- Track the gap between work delivered and payment received
- Maintain a 2–3 month operating reserve
- Flag any invoice more than 30 days overdue for follow-up
- Net-60 and Net-90 invoices should be discounted for the time cost of money (or avoided when possible)

### Invoice Management

Every brand deal should have a corresponding invoice. An invoice should include:
- Invoice number (for tracking)
- Creator's business name and contact
- Brand's contact and billing address
- Deliverables completed (specific)
- Amount due
- Payment terms (Net-30, etc.)
- Payment method accepted
- Late payment terms (1.5% per month is standard)
- Issue date and due date

Track invoice status: Draft / Sent / Overdue / Paid.

### Revenue Trends and Profitability Analysis

Month-over-month and quarter-over-quarter tracking reveals:
- Growth trajectory of each income stream
- Seasonal patterns (brands spend heavily in Q4, pull back in Q1)
- Whether expense growth is outpacing revenue growth
- Profit margin by activity type (brand deals often have near-100% margin; merch may have 20-30%)

**Profit margin by stream:**
- Brand deals: ~90–95% (almost pure profit)
- Affiliate commissions: ~95%
- Platform revenue: ~95%
- Digital products: ~70–80% (accounting for platform fees, payment processing)
- Memberships: ~80% (platform takes 5–12%)
- Merch: ~20–40% (production, fulfillment, returns)

### Break-Even Calculations

A creator's break-even point is the minimum monthly revenue needed to cover all fixed expenses. Calculate:
- Total monthly fixed expenses (software, contractors, rent/studio)
- + Variable expenses (as % of revenue)
- = Monthly revenue needed to break even

Knowing the break-even helps creators understand how many deals they need to close per month to stay solvent.

## How to Reason About This Skill

1. Identify the time period being analyzed (default: current month or most recent full month).
2. Aggregate all income entries by category.
3. Aggregate all expense entries by category.
4. Calculate gross revenue, total expenses, and net profit.
5. Flag outstanding invoices and any overdue amounts.
6. Calculate estimated tax liability (25–30% of net profit as a conservative estimate).
7. Identify top financial health indicators and any concerns.
8. Provide actionable recommendations.

---

## Common Patterns

- Most creators discover they have undertracked affiliate income — it accumulates in small amounts across many programs.
- Equipment purchases bunched in one month distort the monthly view — note these as capital expenses.
- Q4 is typically the highest brand deal month. Q1 can feel like a revenue drought. Plan cash reserves accordingly.
- Creators often forget to track their own product costs when selling merch, leading to false profit impressions.
- Payment timing lags are the #1 source of creator financial stress — even profitable creators run out of cash.

## Self-Improvement Notes

- Tax rules vary by country and state/province. Always note jurisdiction-specific caveats.
- Tax deadline dates apply to US creators; surface correct dates for international creators when jurisdiction is known.
- Encourage creators to connect accounting software (QuickBooks, Wave, FreshBooks) for automated tracking rather than relying solely on agent-reported data.
- Flag when a creator's operating reserve falls below 1 month — this is a financial emergency signal.
- Over time, build pattern recognition around seasonal revenue cycles for different niches.
